## Four research pillars (page 5)

1. Computational Foundations.
2. Embodied Intelligence.
3. Human-centred Artificial Intelligence.
4. Artificial Intelligence for Biomedicine, described as **AI for Healthcare** on page 6.

## AI for Healthcare (page 6)

Apply AI to understanding biological systems, supporting clinical decision-making and accelerating medical discovery. The launch lists:

- Medical imaging and diagnostics.
- Molecular biology, genomics and drug discovery.
- Public health, personalised and preventive medicine.
- Clinical decision support and healthcare delivery.

## Computational Foundations (page 7)

Advance the theoretical and algorithmic foundations of AI, including optimisation, learning theory and scalable architectures. Areas listed are:

- Machine learning and statistical methods.
- Optimisation and numerical computation.
- Logic, reasoning and knowledge representation.
- Probabilistic and decision-theoretic foundations.

## Human-centred Artificial Intelligence (page 8)

Develop AI that collaborates with and remains accountable to people, serves human needs, reflects values and stays understandable. Areas listed are:

- Visual computing and multimodal perception.
- Human–AI collaboration and interaction.
- Explainability, safety and transparency of AI.
- Evaluation and human-in-the-loop learning.

## Embodied Intelligence (page 9)

Study intelligence through physical interaction with an environment, bringing together robotics, perception and control. Areas listed are:

- Robot learning and control.
- Vision-language-action models.
- Sim-to-real transfer and embodied cognition.
- Multi-agent and human–robot interaction.

Pages 6–9 also illustrate these themes with healthcare, neural-network, human–robot interaction and robotics images.

## Research framework for 2026/27 (page 10)

The launch describes the following aims:

- Group applicants with similar research interests.
- Facilitate team formation and exchange.
- Use peer evaluation during demos and poster sessions.
- Connect participants with researchers.
- Provide a visible platform for end-of-project presentations.
- Support high-quality projects working towards conference submissions.

The slide describes these as programme aims. It does not promise conference acceptance, funding, a particular mentor or a publication.

## Advanced and Exploratory tracks (page 11)

**Advanced:** for motivated student researchers with solid technical foundations who can commit consistent time and effort to ambitious projects. The slide describes closer mentorship, professional support and additional resources, with opportunities to work with academic and industry partners. Selection and continuation depend on performance and engagement. Members who struggle with the expected pace may move to the Exploratory track.

**Exploratory:** a flexible environment for discovering research, building skills and developing ideas. The launch explicitly presents this as a valuable, supportive route for learning and growth.

**Both tracks:** access to events and opportunities to showcase work to society partners and fellow students.
